Research & Inquiry

My research asks how we might see disease sooner and understand it better — from epigenetic signals in a blood sample to rare neurodegenerative conditions that are often missed. It matters most to me when it points back to patients.

Classifying Cancer Through 5hmC Signatures

Computational oncology · FARII advanced research programme · Preprint on Research Square

Could a blood test read cancer’s signals sooner, and with less discomfort than a tissue biopsy? My research explores whether promoter-associated 5hmC signatures in cell-free DNA can help distinguish cancer types and progression stages.

I taught myself machine learning to build the study, then analysed epigenetic features across more than 1,000 patient records, comparing logistic regression, random forest and gradient boosting models. The work also examines how depletion of the 5hmC biomarker relates to cancer progression.

It showed me how much patient insight can sit inside data, and how carefully that data has to be handled before anyone draws clinical conclusions.

Read the preprint Research report (PDF)
  • 1,000+ patient records analysed
  • Three machine-learning models compared
  • 90–92% reported accuracy on the study dataset
  • Mentored through the FARII advanced research programme

Research-stage work. The reported accuracy concerns the study dataset; this is not a clinically validated diagnostic.

The common thread

What Connects My Research

Earlier, gentler detection

Whether it is cancer in cell-free DNA or microplastics in drinking water, I keep asking how to see a problem sooner, more cheaply and with less harm.

Overlooked conditions

Prion disease in India and intersecting diseases in sub-Saharan Africa both sit where research and diagnosis are thin, and where good questions can matter most.

From data to patients

Models and reviews are only useful if they reach the clinic. Shadowing physicians keeps me honest about what a result would mean for the person in the room.
